## HKUST — Research Assistant, Atmospheric Data Science

The team had read a paper on using persistent homology (a topological data
analysis technique) to predict US stock market crashes, and wanted to see
if a similar technique could predict phenomena in the atmospheric and
oceanic sciences — specifically the El Niño Southern Oscillation signal,
which is notoriously noisy and hard to predict. The idea: a long enough
signal can represent all the latent and complementary variables driving
these changes. Marvin was responsible for experimenting with this idea,
finding and cleaning a dataset, and attempting to build the model.

He learned that in research and industry, sometimes you just have to use
your intuition to throw ideas at the wall and see what sticks — an initial
"breadth-first search" often works better than committing to one plan from
the start. He also learned firsthand how important careful data cleaning
and preparation really is. He enjoyed working with Professor Julian Mak,
who was passionate about the field and loved making connections back to his
own research.

## PricewaterhouseCoopers — Data Analyst Intern

Marvin's team helped rework a legacy SAP procurement system for a
government department in Hong Kong. Day to day, that meant talking to the
client, receiving feedback on changes, and running regular, rigorous
testing on the product. Marvin helped define a more data- and
statistics-structured approach to evaluating whether changes actually
worked — implementing statistical tests to check for a noticeable effect
from new features, even though the client had final say.

A specific memory: he helped develop a curriculum to help long-time users
(some had used the old system for years or decades) adapt to the new one,
and helped lead in-person training sessions. He enjoyed getting positive,
tangible feedback from people directly affected by the work.

## Hysan Development Company — Intern

A mix of typical intern tasks — translating documents, refining the
quarterly report, naming new LLCs — plus two projects that stuck with him.
First, market and regulatory research on a first-of-its-kind skatepark
proposed for the fourth floor of a mall (Hysan Place): he surveyed
mall-goers and skaters in Hong Kong to gauge actual demand. What made it
fulfilling was returning the next summer to see the facility actually built
and in use. Second, he revamped the company's stock ledger system,
consolidating records across the legal, HR, and actuarial departments —
his first deep dive into advanced Excel, which taught him a lot about the
tools available on the platform.
